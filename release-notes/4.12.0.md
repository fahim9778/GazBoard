## What's Changed

**A Stoic quote on every empty board:** under Happy Inking there is now a short line from Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus or Seneca. Each board keeps its own quote for good, and it disappears the moment you start writing. It is not part of the board, so it never shows in a picture, a PDF or a print. Turn it off in Settings → Canvas.

**Back up all my boards:** one ZIP file with every board, its pictures and its folders. You will find it under Export, at the bottom of My boards and in Settings. Restore adds the boards back into their folders and never replaces a board you already have: if yours has changed since, the backup's copy is kept beside it.

**Board replay:** Export → Replay how this board was drawn shows the board being written again, stroke by stroke, in the order it was made. Play, pause, choose 1×, 2× or 4×, drag the slider to any moment, and save it as a full-HD video or a GIF. Zoom with the wheel, a pinch or the + and − buttons, drag to move around, and turn on Follow pen to have the view glide along with the writing; a long board starts close up, with the whole board shown small in the corner. Even a board with thousands of strokes replays smoothly. Boards drawn before this update replay at an even pace.

**Formula graphs:** Insert → Formula graph draws whatever you type: functions like y = x^2 - 2, sideways ones like x = y^2, polar curves like r = 2 sin(3θ), parametric curves like (cos t, sin 2t), equations like x^2 + y^2 = 9, and points like (2, 3). Each formula gets its own colour and a neatly typeset label, roots, peaks and crossings are marked, and any letter such as a, b or c gets a slider (with ▶ to sweep it) so the class can watch the curve change. Tap a curve while editing to pin a point on it. Graphs stay sharp in PDF and SVG, and you can write on top of them.

**Spotlight:** Ctrl+Alt+S (⌘⌥S on a Mac) dims the board except a soft circle of light that follows your pointer, pen or finger. [ and ] change its size and Esc turns it off; you can keep writing under it.

**Spinner wheel:** type your class list once and spin to pick who answers next. It lands fairly, can take each winner off the wheel, and remembers the names for the next lesson. Find both under ⋯ and on the Present bar.

The quotes come from public-domain translations by George Long (1862), T. W. Higginson (1865) and Aubrey Stewart (1889).
